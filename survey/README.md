## Abstract

This report surveys **Jev-class (System One) single-pass decision models** and the **fast/slow cooperation agent frameworks** they enable. The purpose is to understand the problem before we train such a model ourselves: which works solve which class of problem, and which blind spots we have identified that existing work does not cover.

The evidence base is **283 arXiv papers** (about 70 of them read in full), 17 pages of TypeSafe official documentation, 25 GitHub READMEs, 6 model cards, and 14 blog and leaderboard pages, with a time window concentrated on September–October 2026. **Every number in the report was checked against the cached source text; anything whose source could not be found was deleted.**

The organizing axis of the report was fixed in the 2026-10-28 discussion as "**generalization of semantic understanding**", not the original "business cost". The synthesis of six rounds of discussion is locked as the **discussion draft** (§2.2: what we want to do, the business goal, support from existing work, the defect registry, the final framework); §2.3 and §8.2 are its adversarial review. **P0″–P5″ in §2.2.5 are the final framework statement of this report.**

## 1. Core logic (the whole report is organized around these six statements; full text in §2.2.5)

| # | Statement | What it replaces |

| **P0″** | **Semantics to the model, arithmetic to the code, questions are first-class objects.** The model maps the state into a **per-question** scaled typed observation; the code does thresholding, sufficiency checks and the union bound, and its parameters may only be fitted on held-out ground truth. | **P5″** | **Unit price is an enabling condition; the trade-off has been decided by a design choice**: the fixed order (task definition → input → candidate branches) places the cache on the "same rule set" axis; we do not have Jev's "extra questions nearly free"; what we keep is the one to two orders of magnitude from not decoding; A4 chose the expensive, rule-sensitive side. | "The business goal is to be cheap" |

**One structural finding runs throughout**: in eleven rounds of discussion, the propositions of seven rounds (idempotence is the goal / the core is turning it into code / consistency rate is the core metric / force it to read the definitions / train "capability" / independent scoring is comparable / expert-defined levels, the fourth being my own) have degenerate optima under no constraints (lookup table, lookup table, constant classifier, ignore the names, learn the generator grammar, separately calibrated and mutually incomparable, priors by level position); every time it was "the invariance was named, the discrimination was left blank". When the next proposition is raised, first check whether its unconstrained optimum is a degenerate model (§2.2.6).

**Known boundaries of the discussion draft and the fixed structure** (end of §2.2.1, §2.3): the nine omissions have each been discussed to completion. The questioner fixed a structure different from Jev's: **task definition → input → candidate branches** (cache along the rule axis); the candidate set is declared exclusive or stackable; **levels are defined by business experts** and declared with a type; the model outputs a level distribution and the harness thresholds by cumulative mass; labels are given by the business at construction time and by outcome retrospection in production; the input is a mix of semantic specification + dialogue record + historical context; **the deliverable is a template standard released for business staff to write in, plus a model that can read it**. Three things remain open: (a) denominator data and rule-diversity data for "cross-question re-partition" (zero after local sampling and checking, §6.1.4.3); (b) the hybrid vs attention-only controlled experiment (defect 11); (c) direct evaluation by business-staff authors (defect 13, required before the template is released).

## 3. Core conclusions

### 3.1 Object: what it is and what it is not

1. **Jev's value lies in interface, cost, latency and calibration, not accuracy**. The early audit conclusion from 28 papers was that "typed readout by itself shows no accuracy advantage independent of same-class label-probability readout" ([2609.32160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160)); an off-the-shelf chat model with its logits read directly is already a Jev-style decision model ([2610.02076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02076)). The sharper formulation is not "the value lies in the interface contract" but that **the contract hands the bookkeeping of the decision to code and leaves the semantics in the model**: the step of mapping an open-world situation into a scaled observation has no code form at all (§2.1.7).
2. **The architecture has been reconstructed and reproduced by more than 15 open-source recipes**: shared state prefix + isolated question branches + list-style probability readout. Decision fine-tuned models from 0.6B to 9B are available, and **a domain fine-tuned small model beats a general-purpose large one** (SecJev-0.8B is 20.51 pp above Kev-9B, [2610.03073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03073); AgentDecision-4B scores 91.5% on out-of-distribution tasks against Jev's 82.0%, [2610.21412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21412)).
3. **Backbone constraint**: Qwen3.5 / 3.8 is a hybrid of Gated DeltaNet + attention; the recurrent layers do not recognize attention masks, so a tree mask cannot isolate sibling branches. Existing practice is one row per question (Kev) or prefix forking (8–19× faster inference, but gradients through the cache are not yet implemented). Literature on "training-time branch isolation" for hybrid architectures: **zero hits** (§1.3).
4. **The same word has different definitions across papers**: "decision model" is defined by interface, contract or purpose; "confidence" is four quantities that cannot be merged; ECE has at least five algorithms; "flip rate" has five denominators; "RLCD" has four meanings. This is the root of why cross-paper numbers cannot be compared directly (§1.4.10 lists the six points of disagreement that must be fixed before the problem can be defined).

### 3.2 Semantics: this is the weakest layer

5. **Label names override definitions, while the type is always correct**. A name-definition swap takes mean AUC from **93.8% → 23.2%** (below random), while the **type error rate stays at 0% throughout**; a correct type ≠ having read the definition ([2609.26758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26758)); **deleting all definitions leaves accuracy unchanged** (0.8487), while definitions alone can sustain 0.7971 and accuracy drops to 0.2281 when labels and definitions conflict, with the cause localized in prompt rendering ([2610.02586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02586)); for 1–12B small models in the few-shot setting, **semantic coverage is exactly zero** ([2511.21038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21038)). **No paper directly measures "whether decision fine-tuning preserves or destroys ICL"** (§3.1).
6. **Rules can be read out, but are executed only 22%–72% of the time** ([2610.18579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18579)); on matched pairs containing arithmetic/time, accuracy goes 87.7% → 57.4% from difficulty 1 to 3; when the correct answer is absent the refusal rate is only **7%**, and one added threshold brings it to **79%**; a fallback option drops Intern from 97.9% to 23.1% (§4.1).
7. **D3 already has primitive prototypes to borrow from** (three primitives, fan-out, classification trees, composite scoring, confidence routing; SemTree's Yes/No/Unknown node tree; the "probability-aware, deterministic adjudication" union bound), but **all work on compiling natural-language business rules into verifiable logic targets generative LLMs; there is no rule language for single-pass decision models yet**; and rule artifacts must be **compiled per judge** (changing the judge: 67.70% → 47.87%, [2610.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13999)) (§3.2).

### 3.3 Consistency: the primary metric, but the denominator is not fixed

8. **"Consistency rate" is not a metric until the denominator is fixed**. For the same model: **99.83%** under the byte-level rerun protocol, 0.11%–8.30% under order permutation, **53.7%** under the answer-preserving adversarial-variant protocol (attacks flip 46.3% of correct decisions, and among decisions still executed at a 0.8 threshold 23.5% are wrong, [2609.31142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142)): **a gap of 46 percentage points between the two ends**. Of five candidate denominators, two are already achieved or can be bought by construction (so their value as a training objective is zero or very low), and the only one with business meaning, the "**business-declared equivalence class**", **has never been defined by anyone** (§2.1.8, §7.5 row 13).
9. **Aggregate accuracy is blind to the changes we care about**: across 8 option orders, accuracy changes by **<2 pp** while the cumulative failure set grows by **27–29%** ([2610.35826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35826)); when corrections and regressions cancel out, accuracy is unchanged while the decisions have changed ([2609.27678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27678)). Repeatability itself is solved and **is misleading**: repeated wrong answers account for most of the observed errors (§4.2.2).
10. **D4 is far more than four items**: the report enumerates **30 sub-dimensions** (A same-input stability / B option-representation dependence / C logical coherence / D uncertainty semantics / E input-content sensitivity / F interface and action), each with a quantifiable definition and existing evidence; for the same fact and the same rule, the probability varies with **25 factors unrelated to the business** (§4.2).

### 3.4 System: cost is an enabling condition, not the goal

11. **What is eliminated is the decoding + round-trip + parsing cost**: rubric grading is 16–357× more expensive and 5–350× slower; moderation is about 0.000025 USD per call and about 100 ms, with AUROC indistinguishable from 13 of 14 frontier systems; a local 2B at 30.9 ms with zero output tokens against 8,758 ms for a frontier API. **Only two things can be promised to the business** (the output is always within the option set; one forward pass answers the whole request); calibration, invariance to equivalent phrasings, fallback, unknown, self-knowledge, probabilistic self-consistency, auditability and risk budget: **all eight have counterexamples measured by independent audits** (§5.1.2).
12. **There are five engineering-distinct positions for supporting System1+System2**: the first four cells (entry gate, process monitor, evidence/memory surface, readout of the slow model's output) have positive evidence, while **the fifth cell, "closed-loop executor", fails across the board**. And "faster decision ≠ better agent": the model most accurate on isolated questions solves only 69.5% of agent tasks; delegating tool selection without fitting a threshold costs 19.0 points; a Jev controller raises cost by +19% with an unchanged pass rate (§5.1.4, §5.2.1).
13. **The proposition carries its own gradient**: push determinism all the way into the harness and the model becomes redundant (one deterministic rule over typed policy fields gets everything right; a controller without a model solves **79%** of τ²-bench telecom). So the upper bound of the usable range is how structurable the business fields are, and the lower bound is the repetition of the decisions (§5.1.5).

### 3.5 Learning side: what should change is the shape, not the objective function

14. **The learning algorithms almost never "change the algorithm"**: what changes is the readout position, a proper scoring term added to the objective, and the data composition. What can be confirmed effective is LoRA, data composition, structural isolation and temperature; **reinforcement learning on single-pass models is negative or ineffective** (the published RLCD recipe is 2.5–3.0 points below cross-entropy; REINFORCE −10.2 F1; GRPO no gain), and all positive evidence is tied to reasoning chains; but the evidence base for this conclusion is only three heterogeneous data points (§6.1, §8.1.4).
15. **"SFT cannot bring generalization in the general sense": the strong form fails, the weak form holds**. Transfer is reliable on new samples / new sources within the same task family; but for new schemas (SFT gain about zero at 27B), new symbolic operations, prior shift (the same SFT gives +10.0 or −12.0 on financial sentiment), calibration transfer and reasoning depth, the four layers have **no positive evidence at all**. What moves results across layers is base-model scale, the instruction-tuned starting point, the supervision distribution and structural constraints, **not the supervision objective** (§6.2).
16. **"Whether to use RL" is not the variable that decides ICL and skill injection**. The only two methods that improve the ability to "define the task from context" are both SFT (MetaICL task-level meta-training, symbol tuning); what decides whether ICL survives is **whether the task definition varies between training samples**. Skill injection is a question of **storage location**, and a single-pass readout **cannot execute a procedure** (§6.3).
17. **Task-agnostic RL is indeed alive, but non-differentiability and RL's payoff surface barely intersect**. What RL generalizes is **second-order properties** (confidence, calibration, constraint satisfaction), and wherever a verifier is the reward it is systematically arbitraged; the only thing that is both non-differentiable and uncertifiable is "agent downstream outcomes and decision → action", and that is exactly where RL's measured gain is zero. So "the objective is non-differentiable, therefore use RL" **does not hold** (§6.4).
18. **The shape of the output deserves changing more than the objective function**. Proper scoring rules have hit the ceiling (with known labels they share the optimum with RLCD-style rewards; under annotator disagreement they provably cannot beat cross-entropy). Three of the four output shapes are wrong: boolean **lacks the third truth value** (soft accuracy 0.771→0.978 after restoring P(U)); the choice readout **is ordinal, not probabilistic** (4.2 points of error at the ordinal layer, 21.0 at the probability layer); ordinals are **systematically compressed** (40 datasets × 9 models; scale does not recover it). Numbers should not use scalar regression (an interval decision tree takes MAE from 5.18% to 1.84%), and arithmetic must be outsourced (§6.5).
19. **Chain of thought should be introduced, but at inference time it should not be a token stream**. Training-time distillation is optimal (27B matches Jev on JEVal at about 0.1 s); layer looping is the only zero-token route with depth-extrapolation evidence, but it requires a looped pretrained backbone and is **incompatible with our hybrid base model, with no bridging work**; **reading others' chains is the most underrated** (+4.1 pp under a 1,500-token budget, +8.6 pp on truncated items, saving 71.3% of cost). System 1's own unrolled form is the **question graph**, not a token stream. The hard limit is that Jev cannot make the decisions that "an LLM can only make by writing out intermediate steps" (97.5% solvable in one forward pass, **74%** of the rest), and **it does not know its own limit** (§6.5).
20. **Reading this chapter through the core logic yields one extra conclusion**: untuned readout already reaches **76–97%** of post-tuning accuracy, the tuning gain shrinks with scale from 15.8 points to **2.3 points**, "pretraining provides the representation, tuning only adjusts how it is read" ([2610.15501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15501)). So the question is not "how to train the readout to be more accurate" but "**how to keep the readout from losing the semantics already present in the representation**"; and the only ready-made means aimed at this are rendering fixes (+0.1511) and symbol tuning, **neither of which has been tried on a decision model** (§2.1.7).

### 3.6 Endgame size: the 27B / 35B tier changes how the evidence is read

21. **The endgame target is fixed at the Qwen 27B / 35B tier**, and the evidence at this tier **points in a different direction** from the small-model tier. First separate the architectures: **27B is dense, and Qwen has no dense 35B; every 35B is the `35B-A3B` MoE (about 3B active)**; if the endgame lands on 35B-A3B, then beyond the three structural assumptions of §1.3 there is one more, "whether per-token expert routing introduces new coupling between isolated question branches", on which **this report has zero hits**. At 27B: the decision fine-tuning gain has shrunk to **2.3 points** (untuned readout reaches 76–97% of post-tuning, "pretraining provides the representation, tuning only adjusts how it is read"); **the gain on new schemas is about zero** (87.8–88.1 against 89.1 untuned); **letting it reason is still clearly better than readout** (96.9 against 89.1; on numerical schemas readout gets only 56–66% at every scale, reasoning 98–100%); the strongest positive result at this tier is **distillation**, not decision SFT (Reasoning-to-Readout self-distillation brings 27B level with Jev at about 0.1 s). The counter-evidence must also be kept in mind: **a domain fine-tuned small model can beat a general-purpose large one** (SecJev-0.8B is 20.51 pp above Kev-9B), so "the endgame must be large" needs an independent justification. **Taken together**: what is worth investing in at this tier is not "training the readout to be more accurate" (only 2.3 points left) but "keeping the readout from losing the semantics already in the representation" and "distilling reasoning into the readout"; accordingly, the role of the small-size ladder changes from "a scaled-down endgame" to **a testbed for structural changes** (§6.1.5).

### 3.7 Evaluation and self-review

22. **The "how to evaluate" literature has given its answer, and the compliance rate of existing papers is low**: on the 14-item checklist, "same-backbone label log-probability baseline" is met by only 5 of 26 applicable papers, "option-name invariance test" by only 1 of 27, "two latency figures, with and without cache" by only 3 of 20. **That is, in the published numbers "the credit of the interface" and "the credit of the model" have basically never been separated**. By business property, **the two cells "configurable by business staff" and "auditable" are zero**; the largest gap is the business goal itself: **end-to-end cost per correct result has no reproducible protocol at all**, and one self-audit corrected its own "23.9% saving" to **4.3%** (§7).
23. **Three of the four working hypotheses are "conclusion right, mechanism wrong"**. The reason is that the questioner's judgments come from deployment observation, which is reliable about the **phenomenon**; attributing a mechanism requires controlled experiments, and the literature lacks controlled experiments exactly at these points. **Where there is disagreement, keep the phenomenon and redo the mechanism** (§8.1.1).
24. **Eight frameworks in the report are my own constructs**, not literature conclusions (seven generalization levels, five kinds of differentiability, three layers of idempotence, five System1+System2 positions, the decision repetition axis, the question graph, twelve business properties, D4's 30 sub-dimensions), and the last three carry the highest overfitting risk. In the body, "under this classification cell X is zero" should be read as "**under this report's classification**" (§8.1.2).
25. **Reaching agreement is not passing a test**. Of the seven adversarial attacks, **two statements of the framework were overturned** (the equivalence class is itself a newly added source of non-idempotence; "the model handles semantics, the code handles bookkeeping" holds only when the code's parameters are not fitted on the model's candidates), **three must be qualified** (pre-registration, system level / model level kept separate, credit only in the fixing), **one was undecided and is now decided by a design choice** (the operation that makes it cheap and the operation that destroys rule sensitivity **are the same one**: cacheable independent encoding is about 5× cheaper and rule sensitivity goes 1.00 → 0.24, [2609.37832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37832); the order fixed in the eighth round chose the expensive, rule-sensitive side, and the residual is recurrent compression on the hybrid backbone, §8.2.1 A4 postscript two), **one is a permanent discount** (each of the four load-bearing points rests on a single preprint from within six weeks). §8.2.4 lists five observations that would overturn the framework, and the measurements needed for three of them can be made now (§8.2). After the sixth round of discussion A1 was dissolved (equivalence classes are induced by the questions) and A4 was upgraded to a backbone-specific version (the 48/64 recurrent layers pass on only a question-independent summary); see §2.2.4 rows 11–12.

## 4. Method

- **Scope**: anchored on TypeSafe's Jev / System One, covering the first-party documentation, third-party architecture reconstruction, independent evaluations and audits, open-source reproductions (mainly the decoder route), and the upstream academic literature of each of the five dimensions.
- **Sources**: 357 arXiv abstracts (two rounds of keyword scanning + targeted additions), about 70 of them read in full, **283 actually cited in the body**; 17 pages of TypeSafe official documentation; 25 GitHub READMEs; 6 Hugging Face model cards; 14 blog / leaderboard / specification pages.
- **Time**: Jev was released on 2026-09-15, and the great majority of Jev-related literature falls between 2026-09-19 and 2026-10-26. Upstream classic literature (ICL, calibration, tree attention) goes back to 2021.
- **Number verification**: every number was checked against the cached abstract / full text / documentation. Anything whose source could not be found was deleted or marked "unverified".

## 5. Evidence credibility and caveats

- The great majority of the evidence is **arXiv preprints** (not peer reviewed), with an extremely narrow time window (within 6 weeks of release). Several describe themselves as "early evidence".
- Jev's training details (RLCD, data, backbone) are **not public**; all architecture descriptions come from black-box probes and inference from open-source reproductions.
- The benchmark protocols differ between reproductions; **numbers across papers cannot be compared directly** (§1.4).
- This report **does no model selection and writes no training plan**. Chapter 8 is dedicated to review and attack: it lists my own methodological defects, this report's own constructs, and the observations that would overturn the core framework. **Before reading the conclusions, it is recommended to read §8.1.2 and §8.1.3 first.**
- The conclusions of chapter 2 come from **discussion** rather than from single papers, the only chapter in the report for which this is so; §2.2 is the locked discussion draft, and the final framework is P0″–P5″ in §2.2.5 (P0′–P5′ in §8.2.3 is an intermediate version).

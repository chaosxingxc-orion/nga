# 2 · Propositions and core logic: what this report is organized around

> This chapter is the logical centre of the whole report, and the only chapter whose conclusions come from **discussion rather than a single paper**. §2.1 tests four propositions put forward in sequence; §2.2 is the **locked discussion draft** (what we are doing, the business goal, where existing work supports and falls short, the final framework); §2.3 is an adversarial review of the discussion draft itself.
>
> The four propositions: (1) Jev converts generative judgments that do not guarantee idempotence into "indicator output + deterministic execution in the harness"; (2) functional idempotence is the design purpose; (3) the core logic is the generalization of semantic understanding, not turning into code; (4) the decision consistency rate is the core metric, and right or wrong comes second.
>
> Test results: proposition 1 has a **premise that does not hold, but a diagnosis that can be adopted** (six pieces of counter-evidence, §2.1.3); proposition 2, as a purpose, **must be rewritten**, otherwise its optimum is a lookup table (§2.1.6); proposition 3 **holds**, and it holds by this chapter's own reductio (§2.1.7); proposition 4 **holds but the reason should be stronger**: consistency is the precondition for accuracy to be promisable (§2.1.8). The synthesis of the six rounds of discussion and the final framework P0″–P5″ are in §2.2, which replaces P0′–P5′ of §8.2.3.
>
> One structural finding runs through all of it: propositions 1, 3 and 4 each name only "**invariance**" and leave "**discrimination**" blank, so their unconstrained optima are, respectively, a lookup table, a lookup table and a constant classifier.

## 2.1 The four propositions and their tests

> Protocol: still analysis and survey only. This section answers the first follow-up question: **what is Jev's business goal, what are its properties, which current business problem does it solve, and how does it ultimately support a new kind of System1+System2 agent**.
>
> Conclusions first: (1) **The starting proposition of this section is "the migration of idempotence", but it does not survive testing**: the proposition says Jev adds no new business-processing capability and merely replaces generative judgments that do not guarantee idempotence with "typed indicators + deterministic execution inside the harness". §2.1 tests it with six pieces of counter-evidence, and the conclusion is that **both the premise and the closing statement must change**: the host Jev is itself not idempotent (0.78%–1.33% of decisions change under identical repeated calls, and four papers report its non-determinism), the format guarantee is not something it brings (the existing lineage of grammar-constrained decoding) and it carries a "constraint tax", the harness's own execution plan changes the answer, and capability is not conserved (it is lost outright in multi-step/stateful places, and the harness receives no signal). The version that can be defended is: **it does not eliminate non-idempotence; it shrinks the surface of non-idempotence to a countable, boundable, priceable set of fields, at the cost of failures turning from explicit into silent** (§2.1.4). **Treating idempotence as a "design purpose" rather than an "accomplished fact" is a separate proposition and must be tested separately**: taken literally it has already been achieved (same-request ICC 0.997, same-text rerun flips 0.17%) yet has bought nothing ("consistent answers also preserve errors"; repeated errors account for most of the observed errors), and it can be bought without a model (a lookup table is strictly idempotent; a deterministic rule is correct on all six policies; a model-free controller solves 79%). **An objective function whose optimum is "no model" cannot be handed to a model team.** The testable version is a triple: same decision within an equivalence class + still able to produce a decision outside the equivalence class + a unit price one to two orders of magnitude lower. Translated into model-side requirements this means **monotone thresholdable scores, native unknown, and invariance within business equivalence classes**, while idempotence itself lives in the thresholds and the deterministic contract (§2.1.6). (2) The business goal that follows is **not to make decisions more accurate, but to push the unit price of "a decision" down to the point where it can be called on every branch of a program**, so that a large class of judgments that "were never worth handing to a model" become worth it. (3) In its list of properties, **only two are structurally guaranteed** (the output is necessarily within the declared option set; one forward pass answers the whole request); all the rest (calibration, invariance, fallback, self-knowledge, probabilistic self-consistency) are **empirical properties that vary with the workload**, and several independent audits have already measured counterexamples. (4) The way it supports System1+System2 is not "one fast model plus one slow model" but **five positions that are entirely different in engineering terms**; the strongest evidence is for "outsourcing every external judgment of the slow model", and the weakest evidence is precisely for "letting the fast model act as the Agent in a closed loop by itself": the failure of that cell is directly predicted by "the decision being moved back into the model".

### 2.1.1 The Proposition and Its Prior Work

**Starting proposition** (put forward by the questioner): Jev-class models add no new business-processing capability; they split business processing, which used to be handled end to end by a generative judgment that was "generalizing, configuration-driven, but not guaranteed idempotent", into two halves: **the model produces only one type-constrained indicator**, and **the harness (the caller's code) is responsible for turning the indicator into deterministic, idempotent decision execution**.

**This is not a new proposition; two papers in the literature have already written it as a title and a theorem**, and they must be recorded here first:

- **"Probabilistic perception, deterministic authority"**: the model's output **may only be admitted as a scored observation record**; an admission policy (threshold fitted on a held-out set to a declared false-positive-rate cap) maps the score to true / false / **unknown**, where **unknown means deny**; a **deterministic contract whose sufficiency is checked** then makes the decision. The paper gives the quantitative form of the proposition: **"the probability that perception changes the verdict is bounded above by the sum, over all perceived fields of that contract, of 'wrong-admission rate + unknown rate'"** (a union bound indexed by the contract). A registered study, two constructed domains, two sensor families, 36,000 model calls, and **not a single cell overturns the bound**; perception-caused "deny→allow" flips occurred in 13 of 21,000 test verdicts, all from 3 self-contradictory records, and every flip fell below its registered bound. Note one key detail: **the scores of both sensor families are "informative but uncalibrated"**, which means the bound **does not depend on calibration**; it depends on the construction "unknown means deny" ([2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978)).
- **FlashScript** gives the most precise wording: compile one successful Agent run into a reusable neuro-symbolic program, where actions and data transformations become code, instance values become parameters, and **decisions that need model judgment become LLM calls**; and "**compilation does not eliminate non-determinism; it localizes it, so that the deterministic program and its neural calls can be reviewed and tested separately**". On 120 WorkBench analysis tasks these programs are correct on every run and issue exactly the same tool calls, with about **70 times** fewer input tokens and about **1/3** the runtime of a "skill-guided Agent", whose repeated runs are inconsistent with one another; on compound versions of the tasks the skill-guided Agent's accuracy falls to **23%** and its output inconsistency rises, while the compiled programs stay correct and identical run after run, and generalize to all 108 unseen instances of the same template ([2610.33876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33876)).

**"Localize", not "eliminate", is the keyword for every correction in the rest of this section.**

### 2.1.2 The Parts of the Proposition That Hold

Three parts, and all three hold.

**(1) Decision authority does indeed land in the harness, and this is measured, not a design preference.** Delegating tool selection without fitting a confidence threshold lowers the success rate by **19.0 points**; the effective practice is to **fit a threshold separately for each choice** on the Agent's own trajectories and hand anything below the threshold back to the LLM ([2610.21412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21412)). Naive threshold selection exceeds the target risk in about half of the settings with a defined test risk, Learn-Then-Test in only **7.8%**, and the authors conclude that "relying on a class-probability interface is not enough for deployment decisions" ([2610.19811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19811)). Under asymmetric costs, **using the model's probabilities can be worse than taking its top-1 directly** ([2610.11392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11392)): the indicator itself contains no cost structure, and the cost structure can only live in the harness.

**(2) Every system that actually works does have this shape.** JEVDB treats typed decisions as SQL semantic operators, with deterministic Yannakakis-style semi-join reduction and semantic Bloom filtering in front and the model only scoring ([2610.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02046)); NIOCIS folds execution logs into a loop-folded action graph, where **restricting the candidate set per node** contributes +3.5 points while graph traversal contributes zero within the interval ([2610.25985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25985)); Jev-Guard first routes on an editable policy tree and then does conditional classification ([2610.18326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18326)); SemTree's tree of Yes/No/Unknown nodes can be repaired node by node ([2610.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13999)); Mnemon is explicitly "**rules with an explicit budget** turning a judgment into a small View" ([2609.36059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36059)); CARE keeps the deterministic fast path for common commands and calls the neural verdict only for boundary commands ([2607.21642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21642)); field prediction for enterprise sales orders goes further still: the LLM is **used only in the offline rule-discovery stage**, and inference time is a transparent key lookup that needs no model serving ([2610.34258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34258)).

**(3) The real gain is not "determinism" but "boundability".** This is the contribution of the first paper in §2.1.1: once non-idempotence is compressed into "the wrong-admission rate and unknown rate of each of a handful of perceived fields", the verdict-flip probability of the whole system has an upper bound that is **computable, minimizable, and independent of calibration**, and "which of several sufficient contracts to choose" becomes a valid cost model ([2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978)). **This is a far weaker but far more real promise than "idempotence".**

### 2.1.3 Six Pieces of Counter-Evidence

**Counter-evidence 1: the premise does not hold. The Jev side is not idempotent either, and the generative side was never measured for comparison.**

| Measurement | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Host Jev under **exactly identical** repeated calls | **4 of 510 labels change** (0.78%); in another set, **up to 1.33%** of decisions on 300 questions change between two identical runs |
| Number of papers reporting "host model is non-deterministic" | **4** ([53, 61, 70, 79] as relayed by [2609.32160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160)); checklist item R2 therefore requires reporting repeated-run stability |
| Repeat noise of the open-source reproduction | 0.17% ([2609.31142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142)), **lower than the host model** |
| The generative side | Koa-decision uses atomic label tokens + SFT to **reduce classification to a deterministic one-step decoding problem**, reaching 85.5% on production intent routing, on par with the strongest frontier models and several times faster at the median; against Jev, "comparable accuracy, faster median", plus support for multimodal and multi-label ([2609.36115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36115)) |

Conclusion: **the dichotomy "generative is non-idempotent / Jev is idempotent" does not hold under existing measurements**. Both sides sit in the same band below 2%, and the measured jitter of the host Jev is larger than that of the open-source single-pass model; an ordinary decoder with atomic label tokens is already "deterministic one-step decoding". More importantly: **no paper has measured Jev's repeat jitter side by side with a temperature-0 generative baseline**. So the premise is neither confirmed nor falsified; nobody has measured it.

**Counter-evidence 2: L1 (format idempotence) is not brought by Jev, and buying it has a measured price.**

The early audit lists **constrained decoding as an independent prior lineage**, and in its comparison table credits the row "guaranteed valid output" directly to "grammar-constrained decoding": **"finite-state and grammar-constrained decoders already guarantee that the output satisfies the schema"** ([2609.32160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160)).

And the "constraint tax" measures the price: over 15,000 generations (Qwen2.5-0.5B / 1.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B), hard answer-schema decoding raises schema validity from 61.5% to **100.0%** but pushes answer accuracy from 19.7% **down to 11.0%**, and lifts the share of "schema-valid but wrong" from 49.5% to **88.9%**; on the deterministic calendar tool-calling task closest to an industrial setting, Qwen2.5-1.5B reaches 91.5% executable accuracy with prompted JSON but only **48.0%** under the same hard schema, with both modes 100% schema-valid. In the authors' words, **"the errors are semantic, not structural"**, and they recommend reporting four numbers separately: schema validity, answer accuracy, executable accuracy and the **"valid-but-wrong rate"** ([2605.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)).

This is a direct blow to the proposition: **typing trades "visible breakage" for "invisible error"**. For the harness this is not a net gain: a parse failure is a loud failure, while "valid but wrong" is a silent one. The corresponding measurement on Jev already exists: swapping the no/yes names across rubrics pushes AUC from .815 to .581 while **type errors are zero** ([2609.32160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160) T3).

(An honest qualification: the constraint tax was measured with grammar decoding on ≤3B generative models, not on Jev's trained readout; the failure mode it names matches what T3 measured on Jev, but it is not itself a measurement of Jev.)

**Counter-evidence 3: the "indicator" is not a measurement of the world, so deterministic rules built on it are not portable.**

A pre-registered IIA test of 299,463 calls: **every** added option shifted the log-odds between the gold label and the strongest wrong label, with a mean-square systematic shift of **0.20–0.52 nats**, **1.2–3 times the repeated-run jitter**, even when the added option received no more than 0.1% of the probability mass; the shift is directional; the distortion grows steadily with the logarithm of the number of options, reaching about **1 nat** at 255 options; out-of-domain padding pushes the odds away from the gold label. And **scoring options one by one with independent yes/no questions removes the option-set dependence, at the cost of 8.7 points of accuracy**. The authors' conclusion: **"its probabilities are best read as relative to the option table they were computed against"** ([2610.16655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16655)).

Consequence for the proposition: one **business-irrelevant** menu change moves the indicator systematically by **1.2–3 times the model's own noise**. So the "deterministic" threshold rule in the harness is valid only for the option table, the wording and the set of option names it was fitted against. This is the exact opposite of a "portable deterministic rule", and the only structural fix costs 8.7 points. Checklist item C3 independently records the same phenomenon: thresholds fitted on a choice set **do not hold on held-out items for every fallback model**, and the fitted temperature differs across workloads ([2609.32160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160)).

**Counter-evidence 4: the harness is not a protected zone of determinism. Its own execution plan changes the answer.**

- In semantic query engines, **"the choice of query plan affects not only the cost of a query but also its result"**; accuracy is plan-independent **only under pointwise deterministic policies**; and **when the escalation band is calibrated on the plan's own candidates, selection pushdown is not quality-safe**; "which tuples to drop" is NP-hard ([2610.08089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08089)).
- A systematic review of 139 paper families: **"a semantic decision engine like Jev can return a valid answer and still miss a network deadline, pick an infeasible action, or leave a service in an unverified state."** 50 families claim their engine fits a control loop or a time budget, and **only 4 back the claim with matching measurements**; of the 139 families, **only 4 report deadline attainment**. And the gap concentrates where "the decision has no deterministic computation step": those 72 families make 22 claims, **none of them supported**, and only 2 name an owner for coverage. The sharpest sentence: **"a decision that meets a 10-second budget for every isolated request meets it for no request once decisions queue ahead of replayed execution time."** ([2610.06425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06425))

So L3 execution idempotence holds only under a **fixed plan and fixed load**. Once the plan is selectable, once there is queuing, it no longer holds. The three-layer table in the first draft was too lenient toward the harness.

**Counter-evidence 5: the conversion is lossy, and the loss falls exactly on the class of business logic that most needs to be kept.**

The proposition says "business-processing capability is unchanged; only the carrier changes". This is falsifiable, and it has already been falsified: Jev **cannot produce the decisions that "an LLM can only make by writing out intermediate steps"**: on items the reference LLM can solve in one forward pass it is right 97.5% of the time, on the rest only **74%**; once the answer depends on **two or more updates** from the same user, accuracy falls, reaching 53%–80% at sixteen updates; given a stale state summary, it answers according to the stale summary. Most fatal of all, **it does not know its own limits**: confidence can flag its own mistakes, but not the mistakes it inherits from stale input, and **neither the explicit nor the trained hand-off option catches the latter** ([2610.24929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24929)).

So the idea that "the harness catches the rest" fails on this class of failures: **the harness receives no signal at all.** Multi-step, stateful business processing is not "moved to a different carrier"; it is dropped.

**Counter-evidence 6: determinism is sometimes precisely the wrong target.**

- Collapsing the distribution to one action throws away something measurably valuable: averaged over respondents, **the probability vector is closer to the human item marginals than the rounded answer**, and for ranking respondents it is roughly equal to a persona regression fitted on 163 real people ([2610.14912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14912)); confidence conflates two kinds of uncertainty, human disagreement and decision risk, which under asymmetric loss demand **opposite escalation signals**, so **no single confidence number can serve both** ([2610.33258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33258)).
- But handing the full vector downstream also has a price: full output lowers average downstream cost in **27/27** model-task comparisons, yet pushes the secret-recovery rate on the held-out set to 62.85%–75.35% (51.04%–57.64% using only the chosen class; random is 50%); **confidence fields increase leakage while lowering cost inconsistently** ([2610.23668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23668)).

So "indicator → deterministic action" is a **lossy compression with an information cost**, not a free architectural victory; how much to compress and which fields to expose is a bill to be computed workflow by workflow.

### 2.1.4 The Corrected Proposition

Folding the six pieces of counter-evidence back in, the version that can be defended is:

> **Jev-class models do not turn "non-idempotent" into "idempotent". They do three things: shrink the surface of non-idempotence from "any position in free-text output" to "the values of a handful of typed fields"; thereby make that surface countable, boundable and priceable (the union bound does not depend on calibration; it depends on the construction "unknown means deny"); and the cost is that failures turn from explicit into silent, and that capability is lost outright on the class of judgments that "require writing out intermediate steps".**

Differences from the first draft:

| Key verb | convert / eliminate | **localize, shrink, price** (taken from [2610.33876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33876), [2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978)) |
| L1 format idempotence | Jev's contribution | **The existing lineage of constrained decoding**, and it carries a "constraint tax" ([2605.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)) |
| L3 execution idempotence | Holds by construction | **Holds only under a fixed plan and fixed load** ([2610.08089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08089), [2610.06425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06425)) |
| Conservation of capability | Implicitly holds | **Does not hold**; the loss concentrates in multi-step/stateful places and the harness receives no signal ([2610.24929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24929)) |
| Semantics of the "indicator" | A measured value the harness can build rules on | **A relative quantity with respect to that option table**; business-irrelevant menu changes cause shifts of 1.2–3 times its own noise ([2610.16655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16655)) |
| What to pursue | Determinism | **A boundable error surface**; determinism itself is sometimes the wrong target ([2610.33258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33258), [2610.14912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14912), [2610.23668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23668)) |

**The three-layer idempotence table is still useful, but it must be rewritten** (this table is the report's own construct, not the conclusion of any paper):

| Layer | Promise | Carrier | Status after correction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 format | The output is always a valid typed value | Interface structure | Holds by construction, **but is not unique to Jev** (grammar-constrained decoding already guarantees it), and it trades "parse failure" for "valid but wrong" |
| L2 semantic | Equivalent business situations give equivalent indicators | Attention mask / readout structure | **The weakest layer**. Equivalences that can be written into the mask are solvable (flip rate 8.30%→0.11%, Prism-1 zero flips by construction); those that cannot (wording, tone, label names, menu composition, irrelevant logs) all fall, and **the effect of menu composition has been measured at 1.2–3 times its own noise** |
| L3 execution | The same indicator gives the same action | Harness code | Holds **only under a fixed plan and fixed load**; plan selection changes the result, and queuing voids every latency guarantee |

### 2.1.5 Corollary: First Separate the "Business Goal" from the "Capability Goal"

Most of the disappointment with Jev in the existing literature comes from conflating two things.

| | Capability goal | Business goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proposition | Single-pass typed readout is **more accurate** than a generative readout of the same size | Single-pass typed readout lowers **the marginal cost of the same judgment by one to two orders of magnitude** |
| Existing evidence | **Does not hold**. The audit of 28 early papers concludes that "typed readout itself shows no accuracy advantage independent of a comparable label-probability readout" ([2609.32160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160)); reading logits directly from an off-the-shelf chat model is already a Jev-style decision model ([2610.02076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02076)) | **Holds, and is the most stable finding in the whole literature** (see the table in §5.1.1) |
| Corollary | Expecting a gain from "switching to single-pass readout" has no basis | Expecting "making judgments cheap" to change system design has a basis |

This distinction is not rhetorical. It determines what the **optimization objective** (§6.5) and the **evaluation protocol** (§5.2) should be organized around: if the business goal is unit price, then "accuracy on par" is not a failure but a **precondition**; what actually has to be defended is "accuracy does not drop significantly while unit price falls by one to two orders of magnitude".

### 2.1.6 Treating Idempotence as a "Purpose" Rather Than a "Fact": Only Written This Way Is It Testable

What §2.1.3 overturns is the **descriptive** proposition ("Jev turned non-idempotent into idempotent"). But there is also a **normative** version: *functional idempotence is the purpose for which we design Jev-class models; if it cannot be idempotent, on what grounds does it become enterprise decision intelligence.* These are two different propositions, and overturning the former does not overturn the latter, so it must be tested separately. The result of the test: **as a purpose it must be rewritten, otherwise its optimum is "no model".**

**Objection one: taken literally, this purpose has already been achieved, and it has bought nothing.**

| Evidence | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Intraclass correlation of repeated calls on the same request | **ICC 0.997** (24 questions, 288,000 answers, [2609.36399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36399)) |
| Magnitude of repeat noise | Negation inconsistency is about **5 times** the repeat noise, i.e. the repeat noise itself is small ([2609.33209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33209)) |
| Same-text rerun flip rate of the open-source reproduction | **0.17%** ([2610.35826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35826)), lower than the host Jev's 0.78%–1.33% |

And §4.2 §D4's verdict on this sub-dimension (A1 repeatability) is "**solved and misleading**", based on two findings from the same legal-document study: **"consistent answers also preserve errors": repeated wrong answers account for most of the observed errors**; and after changing the visible assumptions and the order, **corrections and regressions cancel each other out, so accuracy is unchanged while the decisions have changed** ([2609.27678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27678)). So the property "same input, same output" is one we already hold at roughly the 99.8% level, and it has not made the system trustworthy, because **an idempotent error is a systematically repeated error**: the same customer is denied every single time, and aggregate accuracy cannot see it.

**Objection two: taken literally, this purpose can be bought without a model, so it cannot be the design goal.**

The supremum of functional idempotence is a **lookup table**: memoize by input hash and any model attains strict 1.0 idempotence. Pushing further in this direction, the report already has three measured endpoints: after parsing policy fields into typed values, **one deterministic rule is correct on all six policies**; the decision space of bounded Agent workflows saturates, and **a model-free controller solves 79% on τ²-bench telecom** ([2610.24871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24871)); in enterprise field prediction the LLM is used only in the offline rule-discovery stage, and **inference time is a transparent key lookup**. If an objective function's optimum is "no model", it is not an objective function that can be handed to a model team.

**Objection three: what the enterprise side actually mandates is not bit-level idempotence either.** Audit and compliance require four things: **same case, same verdict** (= L2, stability within an equivalence class), **a reproducible decision record** (one can re-derive "why it was decided this way"), **a declared and bounded error rate**, and **appealability**. Bit-level idempotence is merely the **cheapest route** to buying the first, which is why it is easily mistaken for the requirement itself. But it is neither sufficient nor necessary:

- **Not sufficient**: hard schema decoding raises validity from 61.5% to **100%** while pushing answer accuracy from 19.7% to **11.0%** and lifting "valid but wrong" from 49.5% to **88.9%**; the authors conclude that "**the errors are semantic, not structural**" ([2605.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)). A system that is 100% idempotent and silently wrong 11% of the time is worse than one that is 99.8% stable but flags the uncertain batch.
- **Not necessary**: the class of deployed systems this argument appeals to (scorecards, credit scoring) is indeed idempotent, but their licence to operate comes from being **monotone, thresholdable at a declared FPR, and auditable**; idempotence is merely a by-product of "a fitted scoring function", not the reason they are accepted.

**So the purpose should be rewritten as a triple, not a single property.** Each end has a degenerate solution: the optimum of idempotence is a lookup table (zero generalization: unseen situations get no answer), and the optimum of generalization is a free-text LLM (zero stability). Jev-class models occupy the segment in between, so the testable purpose must state all three items at once:

> **(a) Give the same decision within a business-declared equivalence class (L2); (b) still produce a decision outside the equivalence class (generalization); (c) a unit price one to two orders of magnitude lower than generative.** Remove any one item and the remaining two have trivial optima.

**This rewrite does not soften the goal; it translates it into three model-side requirements**, and note that none of them is "be idempotent". The construction that can be copied directly is [2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978): the model output is admitted only as a **scored observation record**, the threshold is fitted on a held-out set to a **declared FPR cap**, the score maps to true / false / **unknown**, **unknown means deny**, and a **deterministic contract** that performs a sufficiency check makes the decision; the probability that "perception changes the verdict" is then bounded by Σ(admitted-and-wrong + unknown), **and this bound needs no calibration** (pre-registered study, 36,000 calls, no cell overturns it). Here idempotence lives in **the thresholds and the contract**, not in the model. What should be designed on the model side is:

| Requirement | Why | Current state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monotone, thresholdable scores** (rather than self-reported probabilities) | The union bound needs only an admit/deny region, not calibrated values | Doable; and the readout is in fact **ordinal, not probabilistic** (ordinal-layer error 4.2 points vs probability-layer 21.0 points, §6.5.2.2), which fits exactly |
| **A native unknown / abstain class** | "Unknown means deny" is the construction that frees the bound from calibration | **The worst cell in the whole report**: the correct-refusal rate when the answer is absent is **7%**; fitting a threshold gets to **79%** ([2609.39496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39496)) |
| **Invariance within business-declared equivalence classes (L2)** | This is what "same case, same verdict" is | **No paper has defined a "business equivalence class" and measured stability on it** (§7.5 row 13); this is a vacant position that can be occupied |

**The last item must be remembered on its own, because it is where this design purpose is most likely to break in an enterprise, and it has nothing to do with model noise**: the indicator is a **menu-relative quantity**. The pre-registered 299,463 calls show that every option added to the option table moves the log-odds between the gold label and the strongest wrong label, with a systematic shift of **1.2–3 times** the same model's own rerun fluctuation ([2610.16655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16655)). In other words: **business staff add one new category to the drop-down, no business fact changes, and yet every threshold moves**. Under the "same input, same output" protocol this change does not even count as an input change, which is exactly why "functional idempotence" must be defined by **equivalence class** and not by "identical input bytes".

### 2.1.7 The Third Proposition: "The Core Logic Is the Generalization of Semantic Understanding, Not Turning Into Code"

§2.1.4 summarizes the adoptable part of H4 as "moving decision authority from the model to the code", and §2.1.6 uses "the optimum of idempotence is a lookup table" to rebut treating idempotence as the purpose. Placed side by side, these two passages **contradict each other**: if "moving to code" is the right direction, its optimum is likewise a lookup table; **the reductio I used against idempotence applies unchanged to my own wording**. So this objection holds, and it holds by this section's own mode of argument: *what we are building is an AI system; the core logic is the ability to generalize semantic understanding, not turning judgment into code.*

**First make precise "what the code gets".** In the construction of [2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978), what the deterministic contract gets is **not the judgment but the arithmetic on top of the judgment**: threshold comparisons, sufficiency checks, summing the union bound. Mapping an open-world unstructured situation to a scaled observation record is a step with **no way to write it as code**; it is semantic generalization, and it is the only irreplaceable part of the whole construction. So the correct wording is not "decision authority moves to code" but: **the model is responsible for semantics, the code for bookkeeping**. The report's original wording would make the model look like a replaceable component; that was an error of expression, already corrected in §1.1.6 and §8.1.1.2.

**The strongest single piece of evidence for this proposition** (and the strongest "the model is irreplaceable" evidence in the whole report): RLCDAlignBench uses **one generic question**, zero-shot, across ten classes of alignment failure, 44 benchmarks and 5 target models, with a median AUROC of **0.886**, **beating supervised baselines** on most benchmarks, at **1/63** the cost of an LLM judge ([2609.29429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29429)). A rule engine cannot do this, and a lookup table even less: it has no "table". On the business-cost axis this evidence would be recorded merely as "63 times cheaper"; only on the semantic-generalization axis does what it really proves become visible.

**But this proposition must accept the same test as "idempotence", and it likewise has a degenerate solution.** A model that gives the same answer for every input "generalizes" perfectly. Generalization is meaningful only under a **discrimination** requirement, and sub-dimension D1 of §4.2 §D4 has already separated these three things: calibration, discrimination and sensitivity are not one thing. So the testable version has three parts, not one sentence:

> **(a)** Invariance within **business-declared equivalence classes** (this is "semantics"); **(b)** discrimination on **business-meaningful distinctions** (this is "understanding"); **(c)** on **new situations outside these**, either be right or abstain (this is "generalization").

It is worth noting that these three parts are **structurally isomorphic** to the triple that §2.1.6 derived from the cost axis (same decision within the equivalence class / a decision outside the equivalence class / a declarable error bound). Two mutually independent axes deriving the same three-part structure is a sign that it is fairly stable; it also shows that **the dispute is not about what the goal is, but about which item is the primary one**.

**The most valuable corollary of this proposition: L2 is the measurable definition of "semantic understanding".** This report has been treating "equivalent business situations give equivalent indicators" as a consistency property (L2), but under the semantic-generalization reading, **two business-equivalent situations are the same semantic situation**; so L2 is not a nicety added on top of consistency, it **is** the operational definition of "having understood the semantics". The nature of the vacant position in §7.5 row 13 (**no paper has defined a "business equivalence class" and measured stability on it**) therefore changes too: it is not an unmeasured consistency sub-item, it is **the core metric of the whole direction, which nobody has measured**.

**And the conclusion under this reading is harsher than under the cost reading, not kinder.** The measurements say: single-pass typed readout is weakest precisely at the semantic layer.

| Measurement | Number | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name-definition swap | Mean AUC **93.8% → 23.2%** (below random), type error rate 0% throughout ([2609.26758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26758)) | Type-correct ≠ read the definition. The model uses the **label string**, not the semantics |
| Deleting all definitions | Accuracy **unchanged** (0.8487), while definitions alone can sustain 0.7971; when labels and definitions conflict, 0.8511 → **0.2281** ([2610.02586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02586)) | The only place where developers can write rules **is not read** |
| 1–12B few-shot | **Semantic coverage is exactly zero**; ICL "adjusts how the input is projected onto stable semantic directions" rather than remapping label meaning ([2511.21038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21038)) | Context cannot move the semantics |
| Cross-judge transfer | SemTree's tree compiled for judge A moved to judge B: 67.70% → **47.87%** ([2610.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13999)) | Semantics do not transfer across models |
| Judgments that need intermediate steps | 97.5% on items solvable in one forward pass, **74%** on the rest; 53%–80% at sixteen updates; **it does not know its own limits** ([2610.24929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24929)) | Semantic generalization fails at multi-step, and **without self-knowledge** |

**There is also one direct conflict, and it is the most important finding of this section: the operation that makes it cheap and the operation that destroys semantic sensitivity are the same operation.** Certo's joint scorer (state + rules + candidates **read together**) has a rule-sensitivity recall@1 of **1.00**; after switching to cacheable independent encoding (candidates encoded once and reused, about **5 times cheaper** with 77 candidates), rule sensitivity drops to **0.24** ([2609.37832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37832), §6.2.3). That is, **the cost axis and the semantic axis are in direct engineering opposition**, while this report had previously recorded the two separately in §5.1.1 (cost structure) and §6.2 (generalization levels) without juxtaposing them. With semantic generalization as the primary item, this trade-off must be priced explicitly; the cheap side cannot be chosen by default.

**For our own continued training, what this proposition changes is the direction of model selection.** If the core logic is semantic generalization, then decision fine-tuning is **not** where semantics gets added: on Qwen3.5 0.8B→27B the untuned readout already reaches **76–97%** of post-tuning accuracy, the tuning gain shrinks from 15.8 points to **2.3 points**, 1/16 of the data recovers most of it, and training only the letter head "recovers almost none of the gain"; the authors' explanation is "**pretraining provides the representation, tuning only adjusts how it is read**" ([2610.15501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15501)). The same paper also gives a discriminating control: the same untuned 27B scores 96.9 when reasoning is allowed versus 89.1, and on the three date-arithmetic and numerical-comparison schemas the readout is only 56–66% at **every scale** while reasoning is 98–100%. So under this reading the question to ask is not "how to train the readout to be more accurate" but **"how to keep the readout from losing the semantics already present in the representation"**; and the literature offers only two ready-made means aimed at this, neither tried on decision models: changing the rendering layer to write only definitions, with meaningless identifiers as labels (accuracy **+0.1511**, [2610.02586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02586)), and symbol tuning (using meaningless symbols as labels to force reading the definitions, which directly improves the ability to **flip in-context label semantics**, [2305.08298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08298), §6.3.2; §3.1 blind spot 2 already records "nobody has tried it on decision models").

### 2.1.8 The Fourth Proposition: "The Decision Consistency Rate Is the Core Metric, and Right or Wrong Comes Second"

This proposition is on the same line as §2.1.7: if invariance within an equivalence class **is** the operational definition of "having understood the semantics", then the consistency rate is naturally the primary metric. This section has three parts: the reason for it that is stronger than the one the questioner gave, the item it must add, and the exchange rate it must price explicitly.

**(i) It holds, and with a reason stronger than "more important": consistency comes before accuracy.**

Accuracy is a property that can be measured and accepted; consistency is the property **that makes the measurement itself well-defined**. The whole calibration-free union-bound construction depends on "the threshold is fitted on a held-out set to a declared FPR" ([2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978)), and a threshold is valid only while the score distribution is stable. Once scores within an equivalence class can drift, **the threshold fails and the bound no longer holds**; at that point the question "how accurate is it" does not even have a definite answer. The measured price: in one paper the threshold was fitted in-sample, **targeting 5% and reaching up to 17% on the held-out set** ([2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)); SHIFT-JEV, with thresholds certified by Learn-then-Test, still exceeds the target risk of 10% in **3–5** of 27 environment-seed pairs (5–15 for max-probability scores, 9–14 for conformal sets, [2610.34329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34329)). So the correct statement is not "consistency matters more than accuracy" but **consistency is the precondition for accuracy to be promisable**.

**There is also one independent piece of support: aggregate accuracy is blind to the changes we actually care about.** Across 8 option orderings for Jev, **accuracy changes by less than 2 percentage points while the cumulative failure set grows by 27–29%** ([2610.35826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35826)); after changing the visible assumptions and the order, **corrections and regressions cancel each other out, so accuracy is unchanged while the decisions have changed** ([2609.27678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27678)). Putting right or wrong second comes with a condition: **right or wrong can no longer be measured with aggregate accuracy either**; it must use the decision-level paired protocol (cumulative failure set + equivalence-class flip rate). §8.4 Q4.6 has already listed this as "needs a standard, not a new method".

**(ii) The item that must be added: the unconstrained optimum of the consistency rate is a constant classifier.**

This is the third time this section meets the same structure. The optimum of idempotence is a lookup table (§2.1.6); the optimum of "moving to code" is also a lookup table (§2.1.7); the optimum of the consistency rate is **giving the same answer for every input**. Each of the three propositions names the "invariance" half and leaves the "discrimination" half blank, so the unconstrained optimum of each is a degenerate model.

And this is not a hypothetical failure: the report has its measured form. **Ordinal scales are systematically compressed** (40 datasets × 9 models, worse the finer the scale, and model scale does not recover it, [2609.38827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38827)): this is exactly the model buying stability with "not using the scale"; **homogeneous demonstrations collapse the accuracy of 0.8B–8B models to below 12%** ([2605.08295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08295)): this is collapse to the majority class. So the usable form of this proposition must have two terms:

> **Consistency rate as the objective function, accuracy as a constraint** (declare an accuracy floor that must not be breached), rather than the lexicographic order "consistency first, accuracy second". Under lexicographic ordering, "second" is, in engineering terms, "unbounded".

**(iii) The thing that must be fixed first: the denominator of the consistency rate.** §1.4 has already pointed out that "flip rate" has **five different denominators** in the literature. Seen by denominator, the current state of this metric is entirely different:

| Denominator | Current state | Value as a training objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated calls on the same request | **Already at ICC 0.997 / flips 0.17%** | **Zero**. We already have it (§2.1.6) |
| Option order permutation | Block-causal takes flips from 8.30%→0.11%, Prism-1 is **zero by construction** ([2610.22066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22066)) | **Low**. Can be bought by construction, no need to train |
| Wording / tone / label names | Partially measured, all fall; name-definition swap AUC 93.8%→23.2% | **Medium-high**, but it is the same problem as "reading the definition" (§2.1.7) |
| Answer-preserving adversarial variants | **Attacks flip 46.3% of Jev's correct decisions**, fallacious arguments without commands flip **77.2%**; among decisions still executed at the 0.8 threshold, **23.5% are wrong** ([2609.31142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142)) | **High** |
| **Business-declared equivalence classes** | **No paper has defined it and measured on it** (§7.5 row 13) | **This is the vacant position** |

This table yields a sharp conclusion: **the "consistency rate" of the same model is 99.83% under the "byte-identical rerun" protocol and 53.7% under the "answer-preserving adversarial variants" protocol, a gap of 46 percentage points**. So the sentence "the consistency rate is the core metric" **is not a metric** until the denominator is fixed, and fixing the denominator is itself the unoccupied vacant position this report has found.

**(iv) The exchange rate is measured, not assumed.** Buying consistency back costs accuracy, and four independent measurements give the price list:

| Consistency bought | Price paid |
| --- | --- |
| Hard schema decoding: validity 61.5% → **100%** | Answer accuracy 19.7% → **11.0%**, "valid but wrong" 49.5% → **88.9%** ([2605.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)) |
| Independent yes/no scoring: **removes option-set dependence** | **−8.7 points** ([2610.16655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16655)) |
| Cacheable independent encoding: about **5 times cheaper** | Rule-sensitivity recall@1 1.00 → **0.24** ([2609.37832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37832)) |
| PACT's pairwise anchoring + ordinal transport | The authors themselves state "**no single component improves raw accuracy; the value is in robustness**" ([2609.35865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35865)) |

But note that rows two and three point in different directions: **independent scoring pays in accuracy, cacheable encoding pays in rule sensitivity (that is, semantics), while by-construction methods of the block-causal and Prism-1 kind have so far reported no accuracy cost**. So "consistency costs money" is not a uniform conclusion: **buying by construction (changing the mask, the positions, the branch isolation) is currently the free side, while buying through a loss term or through serving optimization is the paying side**. This is the same conclusion as §6.1 "structural changes are the only algorithmic changes with large effect sizes", holding once more on the question of metric ordering.
## 2.2 The locked discussion draft: what we are doing, why, where existing work supports it and where it does not

> This section is the synthesis of the six rounds of discussion on 2026-10-28, **locked as the discussion draft**. It answers four questions: what we are doing (§2.2.1), what the business goal and purpose are (§2.2.2), where existing work supports it (§2.2.3), and where it falls short (§2.2.4, the defect registry); it then gives the final framework (§2.2.5) and the trajectory of the discussion (§2.2.6). §2.3 is an adversarial review of this discussion draft itself. **P0″–P5″ in §2.2.5 replace P0′–P5′ in §8.2.3 and are the final statement of the framework for the whole report.**

### 2.2.1 What we are doing

A **single-pass, typed-readout** decision model (endgame at the Qwen 27B / 35B tier, with the 0.8B–9B ladder as the testbed for structural changes), whose interface is:

> **The state is encoded once; every question that business staff write down in words is projected once onto this state, yielding a scaled typed observation (P(true), a distribution over options, an ordinal grade); the output levels (the set of names and its type: boolean / nominal / ordinal) are defined by the business and injected with the question; the code does arithmetic on top of the observations (thresholds, sufficiency checks, union bound) and makes a definite decision.**

The core requirement of this interface is fully characterized by one example (given by the questioner):

| state | Question Q1: "did I eat a meal?" | Question Q2: "did I consume liquid?" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I eat rice | 1.0 | 0 |
| I eat milk | 1.0 | 1.0 |

That is: **the business rule (written in the question) determines the basis of the decision**. For the same state, the two sentences are of the same class under Q1 and of different classes under Q2. This has two corollaries, which respectively fix one earlier difficulty of this report and fill one earlier gap:

1. **Equivalence classes are induced by the question; no separate artifact is needed.** §8.2.1 A1 pointed out that a "business-declared equivalence class" is itself a non-idempotent semantic judgment; this example shows that the equivalence class is not declared separately, it follows from the question, and questions are already first-class objects of the interface.
2. **The discrimination metric now has a concrete form: cross-question re-partition.** The same state **must move** under questions that partition differently ("I eat rice" must get 1.0 under Q1 and 0 under Q2). This is exactly the discrimination counterpart that §8.2.1 A6 requires for every invariance.

But to state this example precisely, three things it exposes about itself must be recorded:

- **"Independent of semantics" holds only in a narrow sense**. It does not need open-world understanding, but predicate evaluation is still entangled with two things: **world knowledge** (milk is a liquid, rice is a solid: this comes from pretraining) and **contestable business conventions** ("does drinking only milk count as a meal?" is answered differently by different business sides; "rice" can be congee). The 1.0 in the table is a **convention**, not a fact read off the text; and a convention can only come from the question text or from examples.
- **It is a one-step predicate**. Real business rules compose (thresholds, exceptions, nested conditions), and the evidence shows that performance decays fast after composition (§2.2.4 rows 2–4).
- **It has a degenerate solution**: look only at the surface words of the question and ignore the state entirely (see "liquid", go look for liquid words). This solution passes both the "within-question invariance" check and the "cross-question re-partition" check at the same time. So the metric needs a **third leg**: the accuracy floor (§2.2.5 P2″).

**The fixed structure (rounds eight to eleven, afternoon of 2026-10-28).** After the nine omissions were discussed one by one, the interface of §2.2.1 changed from "Jev's architecture" into **an architecture we fixed ourselves**, which differs from Jev in three places. First the terminology mapping, because the questioner's vocabulary is the reverse of the Jev literature: where this report cites the literature, "state" means the **input** (Jev's usage); the questioner's "state" means the **output business state** (the level). From §2.2 onward the report consistently writes "input" and "state (business state)".

| Component | Fixed as | Difference from Jev | Direct consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Input** | Supplied by the Agent's context engineering: a mixture of three things, a **semantic specification** (similar to a skill given to an agentic model), the **dialogue record**, and **historical context** | Jev defines only one opaque state string | In the mixed input, "record vs observation" coexist (defect 14); the specification part is reused across samples, the dialogue and history are per-sample |
| **Order** | Task definition (system prompt) → input → one branch each for the candidate rules / states | In Jev the input is the shared prefix and the question branches come after | The encoding of the input is conditioned on the **task definition** (the question-aware-encoding side, the direction of Bongard's third stage, [2609.39111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39111)) but not on the specific candidates; cross-sample caching runs along the axis of "the same set of rules", aligned with business usage (rules change weekly, cases arrive by the second); Jev's advantage that "additional questions are nearly free" is one **we do not have** |
| **Type of the candidate set** | Declared by whoever writes the question: **exclusive** (pick one state) or **stackable** (several rules hold at once). Both exist | Jev has only Choice (exclusive) and Noul (single item) | Exclusive uses candidate isolation + joint softmax (IIA is free at the ranking layer, not guaranteed at the probability layer, [2610.01601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01601)); stackable uses one independent Noul per candidate (IIA holds at both the ranking and the probability layer, but **−8.7 points**, [2610.16655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16655)). | **Labels** | Given by the business when the dataset is constructed; once in production, **business-outcome retrospection** decides whether a decision was right or wrong | — | Retrospective labels are **decision-level** (one outcome for N questions, credit assignment has no solution), **selective** (no counterfactual on the rejected side), and **late** (when the label for rule version k arrives, production is already on version k+2); they can refit thresholds, they cannot directly supervise each readout |
| **Deliverable** | Not the model itself but **a template standard** (how to write questions, levels, types, candidate-set types, criteria and examples), released for business staff to write, plus a model that can read this template | Jev's deliverable is an API | Defect 13 (zero evaluation of business-staff authors; 91.8% of SKILL.md files are defective) changes from an "incidental risk" into **a risk of the product itself**; the template must be compilable and checkable, and compiled per judge (changing the judge: 67.70% → 47.87%) |
| **Size** | Lock the dataset and training plan at the smallest size (the smallest available locally are Qwen3-0.6B attention-only and Qwen3.5-0.8B hybrid; **there is no 0.3B**), then climb the ladder; the endgame may be either 27B or 35B-A3B | — | 0.6B versus 0.8B is exactly the "attention-only vs hybrid" comparison of defect 11 |

Each of the three differences from Jev in this table (order, candidate type, expert levels) swaps one of Jev's guarantees for a property we have to measure ourselves: Jev's "additional questions are nearly free" becomes "caching along the rule axis"; Jev's Choice ranking invariance becomes "IIA separately for exclusive / stackable"; Jev's fixed candidates become "expert-defined levels must be read". They correspond to rows 11, 16 and 17 of the defect registry respectively.

### 2.2.2 Business goal and purpose

**The business goal is not a more accurate classifier** (typed readout itself has no accuracy advantage independent of same-backbone label log-probability readout, [2609.32160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160)); it is to make "**judgments made according to business rules**" satisfy three things at once, which neither of the two existing paradigms can deliver together:

| Requirement | Why it is a business requirement | Generative LLM | Lookup table / deterministic rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **(a) Within-question consistency**: under the same question, states of the same class get the same decision, so that thresholds and the contract are effective and one can **declare** an upper bound on verdict flips that does not depend on calibration ([2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978)) | This is the technical form of "like cases decided alike, declarable error rate, auditable", the precondition for an enterprise to accept automated judgment | Not satisfied (the compliance rate for the same requirement changes substantially under a rewording, [2610.05278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05278)) | Satisfied |
| **(b) Cross-question re-partition**: when the business changes a rule, the basis of the decision changes with it, and by **reading the definition** rather than the label name | This is the technical form of "business staff adjust the basis of decisions through text" | Partly satisfied (it can execute part of a rule by writing out intermediate steps) | Not satisfied (no answer for unseen situations) |
| **(c) Unit price one to two orders of magnitude lower**, answering all the questions in a request in one forward pass | This is the precondition for "callable on every branch of the program", **an enabling condition, not a goal** | Not satisfied | Satisfied |

Purpose: **to make System1 + System2 cooperation possible**. Per §5.1.4, S1 has positive evidence in the first four positions (entry gate, process monitoring, evidence/memory surface, readout of the slow model's output), while the fifth cell, "closed-loop executor", fails across the board. This discussion draft narrows S1's responsibility to: **giving boundable typed observations for the questions posed by S2 or by business code**; the decision authority lives in the contract, the scheduling authority lives in S2.

### 2.2.3 Existing work against the fixed design

Component by component of the structure fixed in §2.2.1: which literature directly supports the component, which is its cost or counter-evidence, and which cell is covered by no work at all. All entries are expanded in chapters 1 and 3–7; this is only an index.

| Component | Direct support | Cost / counter-evidence | Covered by no one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Three things read directly off the table**:

1. **RL is the first-choice fix for no row of this table.** Among the fixable defects, the first choice is interface (1, 4, 5, 7) or structure (6, 11); SFT is the second step (1, 5, 8, 14). This agrees with the conclusions of §6.3 and §6.4: for pure readout, a proper scoring rule and an RLCD-style reward share the same optimum ([2610.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25553)), and RL has independent value only when the reward depends on a sampled intermediate object; none of the defects in this table involves an intermediate object. Where RL really belongs is **decisions about the computation itself** (which questions to ask, how many layers to loop, when to hand off to the slow model; LEV's on-demand depth saves 15–24%, [2610.20951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20951)); that is scheduling, not readout.
2. **Two rows are blocked by measurement (11, 12), and they are precisely the two most important rows.** Until "the consistency rate on a pre-registered (state, question set)" is defined, any "training that targets consistency" is in-sample fitting (target 5% while the held-out set reaches at most 17%, [2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)).
3. **The fix for row 1 and requirement (b) of §2.2.1 are the same thing.** "Business rules adjust the basis of decisions" is technically "read the definition, not the name". So the rendering fix and symbol tuning are promoted from "optional" to **load-bearing**.

### 2.2.5 The final framework (replaces §8.2.3)

> **P0″ Semantics to the model, arithmetic to the code, questions are first-class objects.** The model maps the state into a scaled typed observation **for each question separately**; the code does thresholds, sufficiency checks and the union bound on top of the observations. The code's parameters may only be fitted on held-out ground truth, never on the model's own candidates (§8.2.1 A3). Equivalence classes are **induced by the questions** and are not declared separately (§2.2.1).
>
> **P1″ The main metric is a pair, not a single item.** On pre-registered, held-out (input, question set): **within-question invariance** (same decision for inputs of the same class under the same question; "the same question" includes the same question with a different set of level names that carry no semantics, and adding, removing or reordering **unrelated** questions in the prefix; rounds seven and eight) and **cross-question re-partition** (the same input must move under questions that partition differently). This is a system-level metric; the trainable model-level proxies are the ordinal stability of scores and the rank change across questions (§8.2.1 A5).
>
> **P2″ Accuracy is the third leg, not second priority.** It is the declared floor, measured with the decision-level paired protocol; without it, both items of P1″ can be passed by a degenerate model that "reads only the question wording and ignores the state".
>
> **P3″ Idempotence lives in the thresholds and the contract, and the operator is fixed.** The code does **cumulative-mass thresholding** on the level distribution (P(>k) ≥ τ, P(<k) ≥ τ, the rest is C, i.e. unknown / deny); exclusive candidates use the margin between the top score and the runner-up plus an explicit abstain; no argmax, no expectation. Three requirements on the model side: monotone thresholdable scores (and, within pre-registered equivalence classes, the distance from cumulative mass to τ exceeds the within-class variance), native unknown (currently 7%), within-question invariance. τ is fitted on held-out labels per (question, level-set version, rule version), and refitted in production by outcome retrospection (defect 18).
>
> **P4″ Every invariance gets a discrimination counterpart**, now made concrete as the "within-question / cross-question" pair. The credit belongs to this fixed choice only, not to the principle (§8.2.1 A6).
>
> **P5″ Unit price is an enabling condition; the trade-off has been settled by a design choice.** The fixed order (task definition → input → candidate branches) puts cross-sample caching on the axis of "the same set of rules", aligned with business usage; the cost is that Jev's "additional questions are nearly free" is one **we do not have**: the encoding of the input is conditioned on the whole task definition, and adding a new question changes existing readouts (defect 11, the new item in P1″). What is kept is "one to two orders of magnitude cheaper than generative", which comes from not decoding and has nothing to do with prefix sharing. A4 is therefore no longer open; instead **the expensive, rule-sensitive side has been chosen** (§8.2.1 A4, second postscript).

## 2.3 Adversarial review of this discussion draft: omissions and blind spots

§8.2 attacks the statement of the framework. This section attacks the **discussion itself**: what the six rounds never talked about. Ordered by impact on subsequent work.

**Omission 1: the six rounds never talked about data, and the data form required by the interface of §2.2.1 exists only in half.** "Same state, multiple questions, different partitions" requires training and evaluation data to be triplets of **(state, question set, label per question)**. The first draft of this section wrote "all existing decision datasets are (state, single fixed question, label)"; after sampling each of the 24 local datasets (§6.1.4.3) this turned out to be **wrong**: `typed-decisions` (1,200 states × 5 questions), `akhilaaa3/decision-bench` (586 questions), the synthetic part of Open-Jev v1.1 (48 visible rule templates, three-valued logic, with abstain) and `bev-decision` (56,091 multi-question rows) are all in triplet form. What is missing is two more specific things: (a) **cross-question re-partition**, i.e. competing conventions over the same state (the second column of the rice / milk table), is zero locally; every multi-question set is different facets of the same state or different views of the same rule; (b) **rule diversity**: the full Open-Jev mix has 147,139 rows but only 4,101 distinct question strings, while §6.3.2 says that what decides whether the ability to "define the task through context" lives or dies is precisely whether the task definition varies between training samples. The label-source problem was also measured: among the 6,000 question labels of `typed-decisions`, **35% have inconsistent annotator argmax**; business conventions remain contestable under the same criteria, and this is not an annotation-budget problem. So for requirement (b) there is currently **evaluation data with an accuracy floor, but no denominator for "cross-question re-partition" and no rule diversity for training "ability"**; the former can be generated by pairing existing components (rule text + code evaluation), and of the latter two, convention labels on natural-language states can only come from the business side.

**Omission 2: A1 is fixed only for questions written by people; in the Agent loop questions are machine-written, and A1 comes right back.** §2.2.1 says equivalence classes are induced by the question, so no separate artifact is needed; this holds for questions that business staff write **offline**. But the purpose in §2.2.2 is S1+S2 cooperation, and in the first four positions a substantial share of questions are **generated at runtime by S2 (a generative model)**. Then the question itself is a non-idempotent generation, the equivalence class is non-idempotent with it, and the denominator of P1″ changes on every call. The discussion did not distinguish the variable "who writes the question", and it decides whether P0″ holds. The defensible version is: **runtime-generated questions must also pass through the contract** (versioned question templates + slot filling, not free generation), otherwise S1's consistency guarantee cannot propagate upward to the Agent.

**Omission 3: the example assumes everything is decidable; unknown never appears.** All four cells of the table have definite answers. But real rules do not cover every situation (the silent scope omission of defect 3 is exactly this), and P3″ requires native unknown, currently only 7%. The discussion never stated **what the correct output is when the question does not cover the state**. "I eat rice" giving 0 under "did I consume liquid?" implies the convention "rice = cooked dry rice"; if the business side did not write that convention, the correct output should be unknown, not 0. This means the "within-question invariance" of P1″ must also hold on unknown (states that are equally uncovered must equally give unknown), and this item **does not even have a denominator yet**.

**Omission 4: consistency is defined only on the state side; paraphrase consistency on the question side was not discussed.** Two questions that are paraphrases of each other ("did I consume liquid?" / "did I take in fluids?") must induce the same partition, otherwise every word business staff change changes the equivalence class. The evidence is already in the report but did not enter the discussion: changing the criteria changes 28% of predictions with no improvement (§3.1.3); the compliance rate for the same requirement changes substantially under a rewording ([2610.05278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05278)). So P1″ is actually **three items**: within-question invariance, cross-question re-partition, and **partition invariance under question paraphrase**. The third item is symmetric to the first and has the same degenerate solution.

**Omission 5: the cost account for N questions was never done; after round eight, changed to "dissolved by a design choice".** This item originally assumed Jev's architecture (input as shared prefix, question branches after), and so worried that question-conditioned encoding would pay N prefills. The order the questioner fixed is task definition first, input in the middle, candidate branches last (the fixed structure of §2.2.1), with cross-sample caching along the axis of "the same set of rules"; the N questions never shared the encoding of the input, so nothing was "lost". The two remaining things are recorded elsewhere: Jev's "additional questions are nearly free" is one we do not have (already removed from P5″); the encoding of the input is conditioned on the whole task definition, and adding a new question changes existing readouts, which is the invariance newly added to P1″. The typical value and the upper bound of N are externalized to the harness; the questioner considers them not the focus of this stage.

**Omission 6: rules change, contract parameters must be refitted, where do the labels come from; round ten gave the answer, and the answer carries three properties.** The questioner: labels are given when the business dataset is constructed, and once in production, **business-outcome retrospection** decides whether a decision was right or wrong. Labels at construction time carry no new information (the 35% annotator disagreement under the same criteria remains as is). Retrospective labels are new; what they can do is refit the thresholds in the contract (one decision against one outcome), and what they cannot do is determined by three properties (defect 18): **decision-level**, one outcome for N questions, which readout was wrong is unknown; **selective**, only executed decisions have outcomes, the rejected side or the side handed to humans never has a counterfactual, and refitting on it pushes the threshold in one direction (the reject-inference problem in credit scoring); **late**, when the label for rule version k arrives, production may already be on version k+2, so after every rule change there is a window of running the new rule with old thresholds. The positive item: it is the only label source that bypasses "conventions are contestable", because it turns "does milk count as a meal" from a convention into "which way of judging gives better outcomes"; but this trains the downstream decision, not predicate evaluation, and the two must be accounted for separately, otherwise what the model learns is an outcome proxy. The frequency of rule updates differs by business; the questioner externalizes it to the harness; the real focus is that **the template must be released as a standard for business staff to write** (the deliverable row of §2.2.1, defect 13).

**Omission 7: ordinals and numbers did not enter the "question-induced partition" framework; rounds ten and eleven fixed the form.** The questioner: do not have the model output a number, have it output a **distribution** over states (business states), e.g. a distribution over five levels 1–5, and let the harness decide how to use it (>3 goes to A, <3 goes to B, =3 goes to C), not mechanical classification; and 1–5 are not set by us but **defined by business experts themselves**. The version after testing: (a) thresholds stay in the harness, compatible with P3″; but ">3" applied to a distribution has three readings: argmax flips most easily, the expectation hides a bimodal distribution as the middle level, and **cumulative mass** P(>3) ≥ τ is the monotone thresholdable score that P3″ wants; take the third, with the C branch defined as the **residual** that passes neither threshold, i.e. the unknown / deny branch ([2610.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978)). (b) "A state represented by a single token may be fine" disagrees with the evidence: ordinal compression measures exactly the readout of discrete grades ([2609.38827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38827)), and the score primitive is the worst of the three (Jev 1.13 on akhilaaa3: 80.3% vs choice 95.7%); the ranking of the distribution is trustworthy, its mass is not (4.2 vs 21.0 points), so τ must be fitted on held-out labels. (c) What makes it "not mechanical" is not the harness but a loss that recognizes order: the "mass above 3" from a nominal five-way softmax has no ordinal meaning; an ordinal / cumulative-link loss (Kev has it but it is not enabled, defect 8) is what gives it meaning; K−1 cumulative boolean readouts are the way to write order into the structure. (d) Expert-defined levels move three things into the expert's hands: the definition becomes the only information and definitions are read worst (deletion unchanged, definitions alone 0.7971, [2610.02586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02586)); "ordered" is the expert's declaration, must declare a type with the levels and be checkable by "adjacent-level confusion higher than distant-level confusion"; the artifacts experts write have themselves zero evaluation (defect 13). The training implication: the level set (K, names, definitions) must vary between training samples (§6.3.2), and the harness caps the number of levels (the finer the scale, the heavier the compression). (e) The phrase "confidence state" should be avoided: levels should be business states (severity, urgency), confidence is the mass of the distribution; writing confidence into a level definition stacks two layers of uncertainty and is one of the sources of the 35% annotator disagreement.

The order was a prediction I derived from three independent groups of evidence, not a measurement, and withdrawing it costs nothing; but the evidence that produced it is not withdrawn with it: in the defect registry RL is the first-choice fix for no row, at the 27B tier the SFT gain on new schemas is about zero, and verifiers used as rewards get arbitraged. The "type" column of the registry is the version indexed by business constraints. Read together with omission 6: if "reinforcement" means RL from the business-outcome retrospection signal, that signal is exactly the decision-level, selective, late kind, credit assignment across N questions has no solution, and the degenerate solution is learning an outcome proxy (defect 18). The scope of this report remains "analysis only, no plan"; the reinforcement plan is a separate document.

**Round seven: item-by-item discussion of omissions 1–4 (2026-10-28; the questioner gave four inputs, the tested versions follow).**

*Input 1 (data): survey the local datasets first and judge whether experiments can be done with existing data.* The result is in §6.1.4.3, and omission 1 has been rewritten according to it. The gist is three sentences: the triplet form **exists** (four sets), the denominator for "cross-question re-partition" is **zero**, and the rule diversity needed to train "ability" is **zero** (147,139 rows, 4,101 questions). The former is enough to measure the P2″ floor and several items of defects 5, 6, 13; the latter two have to be built, but what is built is a new pairing of existing components (rule text + code evaluation; Open-Jev's generator already has three-valued logic and per-family held-out splits), not a new task type. What really cannot be built is convention labels on natural-language states: `typed-decisions` measures 35% annotator disagreement on question labels.

*Input 2 (idempotence): this time only decision idempotence is discussed; business functional idempotence is out of scope.* This narrowing is compatible with P3″: decisions exist only after thresholding, so "decision idempotence" is a property of the contract in the first place, not of the model. But after narrowing there is still one denominator that must be fixed, otherwise this item falls into the trap of §2.1.8 again: **same-byte state gives the same decision** is already achieved by deterministic readout (99.83%) and has zero value as a goal; **same business situation gives the same decision** is exactly the "within-question invariance" of P1″, and its denominator is still defect 12. One more thing hidden by the narrowing: **decision idempotence after thresholding is a function of the score margin**. For a state whose score is near the threshold, any unrelated perturbation flips its decision (a substantial share of the 46.3% flips in [2609.31142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142) are of this kind). So what can be measured at the model layer is not a "decision flip rate" but the **margin distribution**: what fraction of decisions have a score-to-threshold distance greater than the score variance within the same equivalence class. This quantity needs no new labels, only equivalence classes; and the unknown interval (§2.2.5 P3″) is exactly the institutionalized treatment of low-margin states. Conclusion: the narrowing is accepted, but "decision idempotence" must be written as "the fraction of decisions, on pre-registered equivalence classes, whose margin exceeds the within-class score variance", otherwise it degenerates into the item that is already free.

*Input 3 (what to train): do not train the model to understand a specific task; train its ability to "understand logic and business requirements" and to give a pre-designed output accordingly.* This agrees with §6.3.2: the two methods that improve the ability to "define the task through context" (MetaICL, symbol tuning) are both SFT, and the decisive variable is whether the task definition varies between training samples; this is exactly the rule-diversity-is-zero item of omission 1, so the data implication of input 3 is **build rule diversity first**. But three qualifications apply. First, "ability" is not a training objective that can be written down; what can be written down is a distribution over (rule, state, label), and "ability" is a generalization claim about **held-out rule families**; the `family_disjoint … 1 ood` split of Open-Jev v1.1 is exactly this test form, and 5,120 ood rows are available locally to test directly. Second, the degenerate-optimum check: on synthetic rule data the unconstrained optimum is to **learn the generator's rule grammar** (a parser), which generalizes to new rules in the same grammar and gives no guarantee at all on the "milk is a liquid" half; on natural-language rules the evidence is that definitions are not read (AUC 93.8% → 23.2%, deletion unchanged) and rules are read out but executed only 22%–72%. So by the evidence "understanding business requirements" must be split into **reading the rule** (semantics; an interface and rendering problem) and **evaluating the rule** (arithmetic / time / multi-step, 87.7% → 57.4%; beyond what is solvable in one forward pass only 74%); the latter half has a structural upper bound for single-pass models ([2610.24929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24929)) and is **out of reach of "training ability"**; it can only be handled by question-graph decomposition (defect 2) and arithmetic outsourcing (defect 4). Third, at the 27B tier the SFT gain on new schemas is about zero while untuned readout already reaches 76%–97% of the tuned level (§6.1.5); this means the ability to "understand business requirements" is either already in the pretrained representation or SFT cannot fit it in; for the endgame size the question changes from "how to train this ability" to "**does decision tuning preserve the ability the base model already has**", and this item has zero measurements in the whole literature (§3.1). So input 3 holds, but its testable form is: *on held-out rule families, the decision-tuned model does not degrade relative to untuned readout*.

*Input 4 (output space): the output levels (1–5 or a–e) are defined by the business, injected by the Agent's context engineering, and not trained into the model.* Consistent with the interface (a runtime-defined candidate set is exactly Jev's Choice primitive, and Hanno's 23,900 rows are constructed this way). But "inject, do not train" lands exactly on the cell in the literature with the largest effect size and the lowest compliance rate: **option-name invariance**, tested by only 1 of 27 applicable papers; under yes/no names the flip rate is up to 70.4 pp higher than the 0/1 control, while random strings as names are close to neutral (§3.1.2.2). That is, the model is **not invariant** to "injected names"; either train it to invariance (symbol tuning) or have the harness **normalize** the names: the model sees only neutral identifiers plus definitions, and the code maps the result back to business names (the rendering-fix approach, +0.1511). The choice between the two depends on whether the names carry semantics: 1–5 and a–e do not, and normalization is lossless; `billing` / `bug_report` do, and normalization means pushing all the semantics in the name back into the definition, which is exactly the part the model reads worst. The defensible rule: **names that carry no semantics are normalized by the harness, names that carry semantics count as part of the question text**, and both classes enter the within-question invariance test of P1″. Second qualification: 1–5 is **ordinal**, a–e can be either ordinal or nominal; this is not a difference of names but of **primitive**. The same level set rendered as Choice loses the order (Choice readout is ordinal, not probabilistic, §6.5.2), and rendered as Score has systematic compression ([2609.38827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38827)); in akhilaaa3's own results Jev 1.13 gets 80.3% on score and 95.7% on choice. So what is injected is not only the set of names; the **type** (nominal / ordinal / boolean) must also be declared, and this item has to be trained (the ordinal loss of defect 8). Third, the questioner uses "state" here for the output levels, while "state" in §2.2.1 and omission 9 means the input; the suggestion is to call the output side **levels** (output space), otherwise the problem of omission 9 is hidden by this usage. Finally, this is the **only one of the four that can be tested on local data right now**: re-render any choice set into the four name sets {1–5, a–e, business words, random strings} and compare flip rates; no new labels are needed.

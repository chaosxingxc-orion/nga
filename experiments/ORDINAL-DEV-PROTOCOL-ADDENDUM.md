1. **Correct the breadth of “length-only.”** The four identical-response comparisons necessarily tie for any deterministic predictor using only the response (including response length), so at most 4/8 strict orders are possible. A constant distribution gives 0/8. A request-only predictor likewise ties on all four same-request comparisons. Both argmax and expected-grade thresholds remain at least 7/8; a tie is a failure of strict ordering. However, this does not exclude arbitrary functions of both prompt length and response length, requested-count heuristics, control recognizers, or lookup. In particular, a response-length / prompt-length ratio can order all eight construction pairs correctly. Passing is a narrow necessary diagnostic, not proof of reading definitions or semantic generalization. Four lexical repeats share one construction and are not eight independent human judgments. There is no significance or population-coverage interpretation of 7/8.

2. **Separate definitions, labels and publication endpoints.** The frozen source is the publisher's `Praveenrajus/jev-bench` validation export, not the original NVIDIA split or a new NVIDIA annotation. Its `score` labels are integer semantic grade indices 0–4, in increasing verbosity order. Grade 0 is succinct, not inadequate; grade 4 is verbose, not better. Correctness/helpfulness is a different attribute. The five export descriptions are condensed renderings of NVIDIA's rubric, not verbatim copies of the entire original annotation guidelines. Keep them byte-for-byte as frozen. The original HelpSteer2 paper, arXiv:2406.08673v1, Appendix G.3.1, explicitly permits equal-length responses to differ under different prompting contexts, supporting these relative controls without supplying absolute gold grades for them.

   Preserve external metric alignment: the paper's reward-model evaluation (§3, Appendix H) evaluates whether chosen responses outrank rejected responses on RewardBench, with category aggregation and optional Prior Sets reported separately. Its attribute regression uses MSE; downstream alignment evaluations are separate again. Local DEV exact-grade accuracy, argmax/expected-grade MAE, normalized RPS, flips and synthetic ordering success neither reproduce nor replace those endpoints. The export's ordinal benchmark is itself a downstream construction. No local derived grade test, including any later TEST result, may be labeled an original-publication endpoint replication without the original metric, population and protocol. This admission does not add such an evaluation to these eight cells.

Neither tiny positive expected-grade differences nor low flips prove robustness.
